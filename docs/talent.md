# Phase 5 — talent implementation

Baseline: TM-01 recruitment; TM-02 onboarding; TM-03 performance; TM-04 learning; TM-05 compensation; TM-06 succession; TM-07 workforce planning. All remain in scope. Shared SH-01/02/03/04/05/06/08/09/10 controls apply; this phase does not sign off the complete shared requirements.

Architecture: existing Java 21/Spring Boot modular monolith, Next 16.4 strict TypeScript, native accessible forms, PostgreSQL/Flyway. Talent domain owns state transitions, budgets and scenario calculations; application owns policy and orchestration; adapters translate HTTP/SQL and published Core HR/payroll contracts; outer configuration owns transaction wiring. No new dependencies or external vendor services. Read the bundled Next use-client guide before implementation.

Roles: HR administers tenant talent and confidential compensation/succession; managers access direct-report performance/learning; employees access own reviews/learning and assigned joining tasks. Recruitment/hire decisions and compensation approval require different subjects AND employees. Existing identity revocation/effective employee access remain authoritative. No anonymous candidate portal or external credential provisioning.

Reversible business policies: one requisition may have multiple candidates but each accepted candidate converts once to a separately approved Core HR hire; candidate entry is manual. Onboarding uses explicit assigned, dependent tasks, a required joining form and existing Core HR activation (no automatic sign-in). Learning completion is a self-attested record with evidence text, not external course certification. Review cycles use configured integer rating bounds. Compensation amounts are annual salary and one-time bonus, currency-specific, budgeted as salary increase plus bonus; approved awards enter a durable payroll intake, not the demonstration gross-to-net calculation. Workforce scenarios use entered annual unit costs with stored worker/headcount snapshots; no invented salary source or FX conversion. Defaults are not legal/accounting policy.

Exit criteria before implementation: persist and reload each workflow; deny wrong role/tenant/record and self approval; preserve input on error; enforce exact revision and stable command replay; record actor/time/reason/correlation and before/after atomically; validate task dependencies, rating bounds, dates, budget and decimals; browser verification for representative connected flows plus actual PostgreSQL tests. Build/type/lint/format and architecture checks must pass before reporting evidence. Production release remains blocked by complete security/accessibility/restore/performance/legal verification.

References consulted: [Workday talent management](https://www.workday.com/en-us/products/talent-management.html), [SAP recruit-to-hire](https://help.sap.com/docs/successfactors-employee-central/implementing-employee-central-core/recruit-to-hire-business-process), [SAP end-to-end talent capabilities](https://learning.sap.com/courses/discovering-sap-solutions-for-talent-management/identifying-the-end-to-end-capabilities-of-sap-talent-management). Public descriptions support connected recruiting, onboarding, goals, learning and succession; detailed product parity and vendor APIs are not assumed. Specific state machines, monetary policy and roles here are application design decisions.

## Delivered workflows and business limits

- **TM-01:** draft/submitted/returned/open/closed requisitions with independent HR approval; applied/interview/offer/accepted/rejected candidates; explicit-offset interview records and offer terms; accepted-candidate handoff to the existing independent Core HR hire approval. Approval creates one real employee and joining plan in the same transaction. Candidates, interview history and offer snapshots are retained. Requisitions hold at most 50 candidates.
- **TM-02:** HR-assisted joining information, up to 30 tasks, acyclic prerequisites referencing earlier tasks, named assignees, evidence and completion timestamps. Activation uses the existing Core HR onboarding command, correct employee revision and employment start date. Assignees are the new employee or HR coordinators. HR coordinator UI is verified; a new employee needs separately provisioned identity/SSO before self-service is usable. No credential is automatically granted.
- **TM-03:** current manager/HR creates worker-specific review dates and integer rating bounds; goals, feedback, worker self review, submission, return/correction/resubmission and independent completion. Closed reviews cannot be edited. Feedback changes remain visible in before/after history. No calibration campaign or multistage delegation is claimed.
- **TM-04:** paginated published course catalogue, configured eligible role and completion-validity period, authorised assignment/self-enrolment, duplicate-assignment prevention, employee evidence, completion and expiry. These are self-attested local records, not vendor course certificates. Expiration is displayed; automatic renewal/jobs are not implemented.
- **TM-05:** multi-worker annual salary/bonus proposals, explicit currency, current worker/date eligibility, two-decimal exact amounts, budget reconciliation and independent submission/approval/return. Budget consumption is salary increase plus one-time bonus. Approval atomically creates one payroll-owned intake receipt and records its identifier. **RECEIVED is not applied salary, calculated gross-to-net, payment or posting.** Current salaries are entered planning snapshots; effective compensation synchronization, full eligibility policy and downstream payroll consumption remain incomplete. Returned plans retain frozen inputs; create a corrected plan rather than silently modifying its historical amounts.
- **TM-06:** confidential HR succession plans with talent profiles, skills, nominated current workers and readiness assessments. Profile/readiness updates and additional nominees increment revision and retain the former values. This does not appoint or promote anyone; the target role is a planning label.
- **TM-07:** selected current-worker ID/count snapshots, baseline/proposed headcount, entered annual unit costs by named planning dimension, exact persisted totals/variance and authorised comparison. Up to 200 selected workers and 50 dimensions; no FX or actual-salary aggregation. Baseline headcount must equal selected current workers. Dimensions and cost assumptions are planner inputs rather than automatic organisational costing.

All writes enforce current identity, server-resolved tenant, record/role policy, exact nonnegative revision, stable idempotency key and bounded transaction. Independent decisions check both subject and employee identity. A tenant-scoped advisory lock plus optimistic revision checks avoids silent overwrites. Immutable receipts, append-only before/after audit, actor/time/reason/correlation and deferred audit constraints commit with each change. Payroll intake adds its own per-source lock, source uniqueness and retained snapshot. Native forms preserve failed input, link field errors, protect unsaved navigation, expose loading/error/empty states and focus the opened record heading. Registers are ten per page; worker selectors use authorised search pages of 25. History is paged by 20.

## Files, migrations and startup

Important code: `backend/src/main/java/com/atlas/modules/talent/` (domain, owned ports, application workflows, SQL/web/integration adapters, composition); published Core HR `TalentWorkforceAccess` and its adapter/role query use cases; Payroll `TalentIntakeUseCases`, its adapter and `pay_talent_intake`; `features/talent/`; People workspace routing; `tests/talent.spec.ts`; `TalentWorkflowTest`, `TalentPolicyTest` and the negative architecture fixture. HTTP response lists covered by decimal mapping use an untyped web return boundary to avoid Jackson casting transformed map items back to domain records. Domain/application contracts remain typed and framework-free. The HTTP mapper rejects fractional integer input instead of silently truncating rating/headcount/validity values.

Additive migrations **V81** (eight talent aggregates, audit and receipt tables), **V82** (payroll-owned intake) and **V83** (audited succession assessment revisions) apply automatically through Flyway. Existing V1–V80 were not rewritten. No new dependency, provider secret, environment variable, broker or cache is required. Use only synthetic data with `local,acceptance`; the acceptance profile is not production configuration.

From the root, with existing local PostgreSQL and scanning dependencies running:

```sh
JAVA_HOME=/Library/Java/JavaVirtualMachines/jdk-21.jdk/Contents/Home /opt/homebrew/bin/mvn -f backend/pom.xml -Dmaven.repo.local=/private/tmp/atlas-m2 verify
npm run typecheck
npm run lint -- --max-warnings=0
npm run format:check
npm run build
```

Stop the local frontend before rebuilding and restart afterward. Run the packaged API **from `backend/`** with Java 21 and these existing local flags:

```sh
java -jar target/atlas-api-0.1.0.jar --server.address=127.0.0.1 --spring.profiles.active=local,acceptance '--spring.datasource.url=jdbc:postgresql://localhost:5438/atlas?currentSchema=atlas_payfin_browser_20261021f' --spring.flyway.default-schema=atlas_payfin_browser_20261021f --spring.flyway.schemas=atlas_payfin_browser_20261021f
```

From the root, `npm start -- --hostname 127.0.0.1`, then `npx playwright test tests/talent.spec.ts --trace retain-on-failure --max-failures=1`. Browser fixtures use unique names and preserve prior records. All former financial schemas and the original acceptance schema remain retained. Local fixture accounts: Amelia prepares/administers, Olivia independently reviews, Marcus manages direct-report development, Sophia completes her own review/learning. Use the existing local login configuration; these are not production grants. Payroll intake is visible in Talent → Compensation and at the authorised `/api/v1/payroll/talent-inputs` endpoint.

[OpenAPI](openapi.json) documents all implemented talent routes, typed input/output fields, decimals, pagination and 400/403/409 responses. A 400 with `fieldErrors` means correct the preserved input; 409 means reload the authoritative revision. Retry an uncertain mutation unchanged with its original key.

## Recovery and release checkpoint

Reproducible read-only checks: `python3 scripts/verify_talent_records.py --schema atlas_payfin_browser_20261021f --output /private/tmp/unique-talent-check.json` and `python3 scripts/verify_talent_api.py --output /private/tmp/unique-talent-api.json`. Evidence files are never overwritten by these scripts. `verify_talent_restore.py` creates a **new isolated synthetic database**, excludes session data, restores the entire selected schema and compares all non-session tables/views; it never drops or replaces databases. Restored audit mutation is tested inside a rejected transaction. The actual drill and subsequent recovered API reads passed; this does not establish production RPO/RTO, encrypted backup infrastructure or object-store recovery.

Rollback the application binary separately from database recovery. Retain the additive tables and immutable journals; older binaries do not expose Talent. Never delete history or edit applied migrations to roll back. A validated database restore is separate from an application rollback. No public deployment, real-employee migration, live payment, vendor call or statutory filing occurred.

The source baseline, [49 talent checks](talent-requirements.json) and [execution evidence](verification.md) remain explicit. Phase exit remains open for employee identity provisioning verification, effective compensation/eligibility and payroll consumption, shared approval delegation/reminders/report exports/privacy administration, external provider connections where selected, and production security/load/accessibility/operations gates. No bank, ATS, LMS, Workday or SAP credential is assumed. Country/entity policy, pay-group ownership, scales, residency, hosting, retention and identity-provider decisions remain the existing open decisions. **Production status: NOT READY.**
